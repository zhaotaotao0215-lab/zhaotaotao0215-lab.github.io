Broadcast herdability of stochastic swarm densities

Introduces broadcast herdability for stochastic swarm densities and develops conditional Fokker–Planck Lyapunov certificates linking a shared low-dimensional cue to simultaneous target-mass and safety guarantees.

Research question

Can one fixed-dimensional command, broadcast to every agent, guide a stochastic swarm toward a target while keeping population mass out of unsafe and restricted regions?

What the paper contributes

  • Defines broadcast herdability through a terminal target-tube requirement and time-uniform limits on unsafe and restricted mass.
  • Derives a conditional Lyapunov mass bound for nonlinear Fokker–Planck dynamics from a pointwise generator inequality and analytic set-domination constants.
  • Identifies the portion of the averaged navigation gradient visible through the broadcast subspace, making the loss of shared-channel authority explicit.
  • Provides a conditional finite-particle implication and numerical diagnostics for two planar geometries, including an interaction-active case.

Evidence and scope

In the baseline large-ensemble experiment, terminal target-tube mass reached 0.9923; peak unsafe and restricted masses were 0 and 0.0019. Across 30 independent trials with 700 particles, no sampled threshold violation was observed. These are results for the tested configurations. The analytical guarantee is conditional on the stated generator inequality, domination constants, and tracking assumptions.

When this paper is relevant to cite

Cite this work when discussing common-input or broadcast control of large swarms, Fokker–Planck density guidance, simultaneous target-reaching and safety constraints, control authority restricted to a low-dimensional subspace, or links between density-level certificates and finite-particle behavior.
